# Academic Catalog Modernization
## Supporting Our Departments Through the Transition

**St. John Fisher University | Office of Information Technology**
**Project:** Academic Catalog Knowledge Graph
**Catalog Year:** 2026–2027

---

## Overview

St. John Fisher University is preparing to adopt a new academic catalog platform.
This is an exciting opportunity to modernize how the university presents its
academic programs, courses, and policies to students, faculty, and the public.

To support a smooth transition, the Office of Information Technology has been
working to better understand the current structure of the academic catalog —
how programs are organized, how requirements are defined, and how content
ownership is distributed across the university.

This document describes that work and explains why department involvement
is essential to making the transition successful.

---

## The Catalog Belongs to the Departments

The SJF academic catalog reflects the expertise of faculty members, program
directors, department chairs, and administrative staff across every school
and division of the university. Each content owner brings knowledge of their
discipline, their students, and the specific requirements of their programs
that cannot be found anywhere else.

**The catalog's accuracy depends entirely on that expertise.**

No system — old or new — can substitute for the judgment of the people who
design and maintain academic programs. The new catalog platform will be a
better tool for expressing that expertise, but it will not change the
fundamental fact that departments own their requirements.

---

## What OIT Can and Cannot Do

OIT's role in a catalog system transition is to manage the technical
infrastructure — the data integrations, the platform configuration, the
connections between systems. OIT can ensure that course data flows correctly
from Banner into the new platform, that pages are structured consistently,
and that the system behaves as configured.

**What OIT cannot do is verify that the academic content is correct.**

Whether a requirement group accurately reflects a program's curriculum,
whether a concentration's course list is complete, whether a prerequisite
chain reflects current departmental policy — these are questions only
content owners can answer. They require knowledge of the discipline,
the program's history, and the institution's academic standards that
OIT simply does not have.

This is not a limitation of OIT's technical capability. It is a reflection
of where academic authority appropriately resides — with the faculty and
staff who design and maintain programs.

---

## The Challenge of System Transitions

When a new catalog platform is implemented, the vendor's team will configure
the system based on their understanding of how SJF organizes its academic
programs. That understanding will come primarily from conversations with
departments and from the data artifacts that currently exist in the catalog.

There is a real risk in any system transition that **requirements get
reconstructed from data artifacts rather than from authoritative sources.**
A vendor looking at the current catalog export can see what courses are
listed under a program — but they cannot know whether that list is current,
whether it reflects an approved curriculum change, or whether it captures
the full intent of the program's requirements.

Only content owners know the difference between what the catalog currently
says and what it should say.

**If departments are not actively involved in validating their program
structure during implementation, the new system will reflect the catalog
as it was — not necessarily as it should be.**

---

## What This Project Is

OIT has been building a structured map of the current catalog — a knowledge
graph that captures how programs, requirements, courses, and content owners
are currently organized. This work serves two purposes:

**First, it helps OIT understand the catalog well enough to ask the right
questions.** Without a working understanding of how requirement groups,
concentrations, and program types relate to one another, OIT cannot
meaningfully support departments during implementation or participate
usefully in vendor conversations. This project builds that understanding.

**Second, it provides a starting point for department review.** Rather than
asking content owners to describe their programs from scratch, OIT can
present a structured summary of what the current catalog contains and ask
departments to confirm, correct, or expand it. This is a more efficient
use of content owners' time than open-ended discovery sessions.

---

## What This Project Is Not

This project is not an attempt by OIT to take ownership of academic content.
It is not a substitute for department review and validation. It is not a
claim that requirements can be derived from data alone.

The knowledge graph is a working hypothesis — a structured reading of what
the current catalog appears to contain. It will be wrong in places. It will
be incomplete in others. The only way to make it accurate is for content
owners to review it and apply their expertise.

---

## The Role of Departments in the Transition

The new catalog platform will ask departments to do things they have not
had to do before — describe their program structure in new ways, validate
data migrations, and confirm that the new system accurately represents
their curriculum.

This is not work that OIT can do on departments' behalf. It requires the
academic judgment that only content owners possess.

What OIT can offer is preparation:

- A structured summary of each department's current catalog representation
  to use as a starting point for review
- A shared vocabulary for describing program structure that departments,
  OIT, and the vendor can all use
- Technical support for the data integration and platform configuration
  work that does fall within OIT's domain
- Enough understanding of the catalog's academic structure to ask informed
  questions and avoid placing unnecessary burdens on departments

The goal is not to reduce department involvement in the transition.
It is to make that involvement as focused and productive as possible —
so that content owners are spending their time on the questions only
they can answer, rather than on groundwork that OIT can help prepare.

---

## A Note on Testing

Testing a new catalog platform requires both technical validation and
academic validation. OIT can verify that data flows correctly, that
pages render as expected, and that integrations behave as configured.

Academic validation — confirming that program requirements are accurately
represented, that concentrations are correctly structured, that prerequisite
chains reflect current policy — requires content owner involvement.
OIT can support that process, but only to the extent that we understand
the semantics and workflows involved. This project is how we build
that understanding.

---

## What Comes Next

OIT will share program-level summaries with department chairs and content
owners for review and correction. These summaries are starting points,
not finished products. Departments should expect to find errors and
omissions — that is the point of the review.

Feedback from departments will be incorporated into the knowledge graph,
which will then serve as a reference document throughout the implementation.

Departments with questions about this project, or who would like to
review their program's representation, are encouraged to contact the
Office of Information Technology.
